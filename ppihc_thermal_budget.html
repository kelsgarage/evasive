Methodology — Pikes Peak specifics read first

One-way model: no cooling laps — every kilometer adds heat. Cooling rate from convection is approximate: base 0.3 °C/km at sea level, increases at altitude (thinner air = less drag heating but also less cooling per unit time; net effect is small).

Pressure model: ambient pressure drops from 1010 mbar (start) to 620 mbar (summit) — that's 5.5 psi of ambient drop. Tire sealed = gauge pressure rises by both heat (gas law on absolute) AND ambient drop. A 23 psi cold-start tire reads ~28 psi at summit even with no heating, just from altitude.

Segment heat-gen: lower section is highest speed → more hysteresis cycling per km. Upper section is lower-speed turns with thinner air for cooling. Model assumes heat-gen-per-km scales with v² normalized by section.

Ambient cooling: air gets colder with altitude (configurable lapse rate). This INCREASES convective heat removal per km — partial offset to the one-way accumulation. But thinner air reduces mass flow, so the effect is bounded.

What this tool is not: not a substitute for sector-by-sector simulation. Uses three-segment averages. Doesn't capture sustained braking events (Glen Cove brake section), which can spike bead/wheel temps independently. Validate against in-car TTPMS during practice runs.
